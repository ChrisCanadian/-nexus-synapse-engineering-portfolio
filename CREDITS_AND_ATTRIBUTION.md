# Credits and Attribution

This document is the canonical public provenance policy for the Nexus Synapse engineering portfolio and its bounded public repositories.

It records authorship and material lineage. It does not replace a repository license, grant permission, provide legal advice, or turn a cited source into a Nexus authority.

## Project authority and authorship

**Christopher Campbell** is the human creator and maintainer of Nexus Synapse and the local public artifacts identified in this portfolio, except where a repository or third-party component states otherwise.

AI systems have assisted with implementation, debugging, research, reconstruction, review, and documentation. That assistance does not transfer human project authority, ownership, or licensing responsibility to a model or provider. Material third-party authorship and license obligations remain controlling regardless of whether an AI tool helped locate or adapt the material.

## Nexus relationship labels

Use the narrowest label supported by evidence:

| Label | Nexus use |
|---|---|
| **Original** | Locally authored material, while preserving any material external lineage. |
| **Reference / inspiration** | A source informed framing or direction but was not copied or established as an official derivative. |
| **Adaptation / operational derivation** | A source or local Nexus responsibility was deliberately re-expressed for a new bounded artifact. State both the donor relationship and the local implementation. |
| **Historical reconstruction** | A modern executable artifact is constrained by surviving historical evidence but is not represented as original bytes. |
| **Composite** | Several sources materially shaped the local result. Credit the contributors and the local synthesis. |
| **Pending / unclear** | Authorship, permission, or lineage is unresolved. Preserve the uncertainty rather than inventing a clean history. |

These are provenance descriptions, not legal classifications or novelty claims.

## Minimum local record

A public artifact should record only fields that materially affect interpretation, permission, or a claim:

- origin and author;
- relationship to Nexus or an external source;
- what was absorbed, adapted, reconstructed, or deliberately excluded;
- the local author/maintainer and canonical destination;
- known license or permission scope, including what it does not cover;
- external dependencies or services that remain separately owned;
- evidence status and claim ceiling;
- unresolved provenance or permission questions.

Unknown is an acceptable result. Public availability, access, discussion, attribution, or silence is not a substitute for permission.

## Application across the public repositories

- This portfolio carries the full policy and the cross-repository evidence map.
- Each executable satellite carries a compact `ATTRIBUTION.md` tied to its actual lineage and license.
- Historical reconstructions identify their evidence basis, reconstruction decisions, exclusions, and non-original material.
- Exact mirrors, if ever published, should preserve canonical location, version, and fingerprint rather than being treated as a local adaptation.
- Third-party libraries, models, providers, protocols, and services remain subject to their own licenses and terms.
- A repository's license applies only to material the stated licensor has the right to license in that repository. It does not silently extend to private Nexus code, data, prompts, schemas, deployment material, or external components.

## Publication check

Before publishing or materially updating a public artifact:

1. Confirm the human author and current local authority.
2. Identify material internal and external lineage.
3. Choose the narrowest relationship label.
4. State what changed locally and what was excluded.
5. Check the actual permission and license scope.
6. Preserve evidence status, uncertainty, and claim ceilings.
7. Remove private, user, credential, operational, or security-sensitive material.
8. Verify links, hashes, versions, and tests where the claim depends on them.
9. Avoid implying endorsement, partnership, adoption, certification, or shared ownership.

The intended outcome is a recoverable provenance trail, not a long attribution footer copied indiscriminately into every file.

## Claim ceiling

This policy documents Nexus's public attribution practice. It does not establish that every historical source has been recovered, that every third-party claim has been independently verified, that cited material is reusable, or that any external person or project endorses Nexus.

Where this policy and a repository-specific record differ, the more specific and better-evidenced record governs that artifact. Applicable licenses and permissions always remain controlling.
